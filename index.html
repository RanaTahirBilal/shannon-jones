The work Health care, close to the ground

Shannon Jones joined the Austin/Travis County Health and Human Services Department in 1999, rose to Deputy Director, and retired in April 2017 as Director of Austin Public Health. The Travis County Commissioners Court then appointed him to the Central Health Board of Managers, where he served for eight years, pressing for care in the communities with the least access to it.

Health equity

Program services and structures built for people of color, women, LGBTQIA residents and others facing the widest health disparities.

Community outreach

Door to door, church to church and meeting to meeting, so that residents are heard before decisions are made about their care.

Eastern Travis County

Services for medically underserved areas, including the Hornsby Bend and Del Valle health and wellness centers.

Men's health

Clinical services delivered through a partnership with the Black Men's Health Clinic, focused on men of color and their access to care.

Tuberculosis

Chair of the CDC Advisory Council for the Elimination of Tuberculosis from 2011 to 2014, with national work on TB disparities.

Mentoring

Encouraging young men of color, and many others, toward higher education, good careers and service to their communities.

Where it comes from

Nashville, and a family in the Civil Rights movement

Born in Nashville, Tennessee, Shannon Jones grew up in a family where service was expected. His parents were active in the Civil Rights movement and his grandfather was a leader in that era. He credits them, and the young people he has mentored and helped raise, for a lifetime of investing in others.

Career From the classroom to the county

Before public health, he spent much of his working life in higher education: in administration at several Historically Black Colleges and Universities, as a professor of public administration at an Atlanta university, and at Lincoln University in Pennsylvania, where he was assistant to the president and taught political science.
